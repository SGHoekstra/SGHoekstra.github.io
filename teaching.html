Teaching

I lecture the graduate course Behavioural Macro and Finance at the University of Amsterdam and have tutored undergraduate microeconomics and linear algebra. I like to give students working code alongside the theory, so that models become something they can run, break and extend.

Behavioural Macro and Finance

MSc, University of Amsterdam  ·  Lecturer  ·  2025

A computational course on agent-based macroeconomics. Students work through a sequence of Pluto notebooks in Julia, built on BeforeIT.jl, and move from running a pre-calibrated model of a whole economy to extending it and estimating its behavioural parameters. All material is on GitHub.

Notebooks

  • Julia and Pluto primer
    Variables, types and the reactive notebook model, enough Julia to read the rest.
  • Quickstart
    Load the fully calibrated Austria 2010Q1 parametrisation and run the model for the first time.
  • Model anatomy
    Who the agents are, and reproducing the roughly 9.9 million agents of the Austrian economy from the model itself.
  • Calibration and data
    Almost every parameter is read straight from national accounts. Only the AR(1) expectations and the Taylor rule are estimated, which is the opposite of a DSGE model.
  • Shocks and cascades
    Design a shock with the interactive controls and watch the GDP impulse response propagate.
  • Expectations and policy
    Compare AR(1) and backward-looking expectations, and see what the choice does to policy.
  • Forecasting
    Monte Carlo fan charts, asking what the model would have predicted from 2010Q1.
  • Extending the model: CANVAS
    Implement the CANVAS extension on top of the base model.
  • Simulation-based calibration
    Estimate behavioural parameters with sequential neural posterior estimation.
  • Cheatsheet
    A quick reference to keep open in a second tab.

Running the notebooks

Julia 1.10 or later. From the pluto_tutorial folder, install the environment once, then launch Pluto and open the notebooks in order.

julia setup.jl
julia --project=@. -t auto -e 'using Pluto; Pluto.run()'
