Papers & software

My research views macroeconomics through the lens of complex systems. I have worked with both heterogeneous-agent equilibrium models and large-scale agent-based models, and my aim is to use the insights of both to better understand macroeconomic phenomena, using theory, large-scale computational modelling and empirical methods.

Forecasting with a Dutch Agent-Based Model and Optimally Behaving Households

Steven Hoekstra, Cars Hommes and Frank Pijpers

Working paper

  • Paper (PDF)
Abstract

We develop an agent-based model of the Dutch economy that embeds a HANK-style household sector with heterogeneous marginal propensities to consume. Building on the stock-flow consistent framework of Poledna et al. (2023), we replace the constant marginal propensity to consume heuristic with forward-looking households who optimise consumption, savings and investments over time as in Kaplan et al. (2018). Policy functions are computed using an amortised neural-network method that enables efficient recalculation across time periods and Monte Carlo simulations without re-solving the household problem. Following Adam and Marcet (2011), households are internally rational, optimising given beliefs, but form expectations using adaptive AR(1) learning rules rather than model-consistent forecasts. We calibrate the model to Eurostat national accounts and administrative microdata from Statistics Netherlands, targeting empirically realistic MPC heterogeneity driven by the joint distribution of income, illiquid wealth, and liquid wealth. In rolling out-of-sample forecasting exercises over 2010–2019, we compare the optimal-consumption ABM against the original model, ten heuristic variants spanning static rules, habit formation, buffer-stock target wealth, capital-return MPC asymmetry, and Merton portfolio choice, and benchmark time-series models. The optimal-consumption ABM achieves household consumption forecast improvements of 10–22% at the 12-quarter horizon relative to univariate benchmarks depending on the expectation mechanism (Diebold–Mariano p < 0.01), and up to 28% relative to the VAR(1) under AR expectations. A channel decomposition isolating the portfolio-choice and capital-return channels reveals that portfolio choice accounts for the majority of the OC-ABM's residual advantage over a buffer-stock heuristic (+11.7 pp on consumption RMSFE), while capital-return MPC asymmetry contributes negligibly; the OC-ABM remains the only variant whose consumption gain is statistically significant under every expectation mechanism, consistent with Euler-equation discipline operating through reduced forecast variance rather than higher central tendency. GDP improvements of 11–15% are directionally positive across all mechanisms but not statistically significant. These results demonstrate the value of combining microfounded household behaviour with the flexibility of agent-based modelling for macroeconomic forecasting.

BeforeIT.jl: High-Performance Agent-Based Macroeconomics Made Easy

Adriano Meligrana, Mitja Devetak, Steven Hoekstra, Sebastian Poledna, Oliver Reiter and Aldo Glielmo

Preprint

  • Package
Abstract

BeforeIT is an open-source software for building and simulating state-of-the-art macroeconomic agent-based models (macro ABMs) based on the recently introduced macro ABM developed in [1] and here referred to as the base model. Written in Julia, it combines extraordinary computational efficiency with user-friendliness and extensibility. We present the main structure of the software, demonstrate its ease of use with illustrative examples, and benchmark its performance. Our benchmarks demonstrate that the base model built with BeforeIT is orders of magnitude faster than its original Matlab implementation, while achieving performance comparable to a highly optimized HPC implementation. BeforeIT is designed to facilitate reproducibility, extensibility, and experimentation. As the first open-source, industry-grade software to build macro ABMs, BeforeIT can significantly foster collaboration and innovation in the field of agent-based macroeconomic modelling. The package, along with its documentation, is freely available at https://github.com/bancaditalia/BeforeIT.jl under the AGPL-3.0.

Estimation, Forecasting and Parameter Constancy in a Dutch Agent-Based Model

Steven Hoekstra

Draft

Abstract

Large macroeconomic agent-based models contain behavioural coefficients that are commonly fixed by calibration and therefore remain hidden from direct empirical scrutiny. We organise the analysis of CANVAS, recalibrated for the Netherlands, around estimation, forecasting and parameter constancy. First, neural posterior estimation turns these calibrated constants into an estimable behavioural vector for each of 15 pricing, wage and expectation specifications. The fitted locations provide plug-in vectors for forecasting, but not calibrated intervals or a ladder-wide point-identification result: simulation-based calibration rejects 49 of 222 parameter–cell pairs, and the supply-side pass-through parameter is uninformative by construction. Second, we assess whether replacing unit pass-through and unit wage indexation matters for forecasts. In the current tables, the unestimated corner has GDP-deflator RMSE ratios of 2.28 in the tranquil holdout and 1.34 during the 2020–2024 episode relative to a level AR(1); 14 of 15 estimated extensions improve on it in the first window and 15 of 15 in the second. 4 extensions beat the AR(1) in both windows before the usability screen, including the two headline CANVAS arms at 0.68 and 0.75 in the holdout and 0.80 and 0.81 during the episode. These comparisons remain provisional until the exogenous-input smoother is rerun using only information available at each origin. A posterior-versus-prior predictive comparison is also pending, so improvement over the unit corner is not yet attributable entirely to the observed data. The final stage specifies a calm-versus-bout parameter-constancy test, using disjoint calm fits as a placebo distribution for estimator and sample variation.

An Agent-Based Model of the Housing Market

Steven Hoekstra

Draft

Summary

What are the long-run macroeconomic costs of the Dutch housing crisis, and do recent policies ease or deepen them? I build PolderABM, a stock-flow consistent agent-based model that runs the Netherlands at 1:100 scale, with roughly 79,000 simulated households across some 3,700 neighbourhood clusters, a spatially explicit labour market with commuting costs, a three-tier mortgage approval system, endogenous construction and a pension fund. Housing frictions reach the wider economy through spatial labour misallocation, tenure distortion and pension-driven yield-curve feedback. Counterfactual experiments cover the Affordable Rent Act, zoning liberalisation and abolition of the mortgage interest deduction.
