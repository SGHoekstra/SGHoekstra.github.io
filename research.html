Research

Papers & software

My research examines how heterogeneous households and firms shape policy transmission, and when estimated models support reliable intervention predictions. I combine agent-based and heterogeneous-agent equilibrium models with register microdata, neural-network solution methods and simulation-based inference.

Forecasting with a Dutch Agent-Based Model and Optimally Behaving Households

Steven Hoekstra, Cars Hommes and Frank Pijpers

Working paper

  • Paper (PDF)
  • Award announcement
Summary

We embed a two-asset HANK household problem in a stock-flow-consistent agent-based model of the Dutch economy. Households optimise consumption and portfolio decisions under subjective market beliefs, drawing on Adam and Marcet's approach. An amortised neural-network solver makes these decisions computationally feasible across simulations. Calibration uses national accounts and administrative microdata from Statistics Netherlands, retaining heterogeneity in income, liquid wealth and illiquid wealth. Rolling out-of-sample forecasts compare the model with heuristic household rules and time-series benchmarks under four expectation specifications. At the twelve-quarter horizon, root mean squared forecast errors of aggregate household consumption are 9.9–21.8% lower than matched univariate benchmarks. Comparisons identify portfolio adjustment as an important mechanism; simpler behavioural rules remain competitive, and gains concentrate at longer horizons. The paper examines when optimising households improve macroeconomic forecasts and which decision mechanisms account for those gains.

BeforeIT.jl: High-Performance Agent-Based Macroeconomics Made Easy

Adriano Meligrana, Mitja Devetak, Steven Hoekstra, Sebastian Poledna, Oliver Reiter and Aldo Glielmo

Preprint

  • Package
Abstract

BeforeIT is an open-source software for building and simulating state-of-the-art macroeconomic agent-based models (macro ABMs) based on the recently introduced macro ABM developed in [1] and here referred to as the base model. Written in Julia, it combines extraordinary computational efficiency with user-friendliness and extensibility. We present the main structure of the software, demonstrate its ease of use with illustrative examples, and benchmark its performance. Our benchmarks demonstrate that the base model built with BeforeIT is orders of magnitude faster than its original Matlab implementation, while achieving performance comparable to a highly optimized HPC implementation. BeforeIT is designed to facilitate reproducibility, extensibility, and experimentation. As the first open-source, industry-grade software to build macro ABMs, BeforeIT can significantly foster collaboration and innovation in the field of agent-based macroeconomic modelling. The package, along with its documentation, is freely available at https://github.com/bancaditalia/BeforeIT.jl under the AGPL-3.0.

When Do Estimated Macroeconomic Models Misread Wage–Price Transmission?

Steven Hoekstra

Work in progress

Summary

Can an estimated macroeconomic model fit historical data while misreading wage–price transmission? This project uses neural simulation-based inference in a Dutch ABM to examine misspecification and identification. Known-truth experiments compare wage-setting and labour-market mechanisms, testing whether historical fit conceals distorted parameters or intervention responses and whether posterior predictive checks detect these failures. The empirical application compares alternative specifications during the Dutch 2022 inflation episode, assessing rejection by the data and sensitivity of the conclusions. It builds on earlier estimation work at De Nederlandsche Bank; the redesigned analysis is ongoing.

Shortage or Misallocation? The Welfare Loss from Dutch Housing Policy, 2015–2035

Steven Hoekstra

Work in progress

Summary

How much of the welfare loss from the Dutch housing crisis reflects too few homes, and how much reflects households being poorly matched to the existing stock? I am extending the Poledna macroeconomic ABM, implemented in BeforeIT.jl, with spatial housing markets, job search and job-to-job mobility in the tradition of Postel-Vinay and Robin, demography, firm entry and exit, and endogenous growth. Housing mechanisms include rent and mortgage lock-in, underwriting constraints and construction delays. The project is designed to separate shortage from misallocation over 2015–2035 and compare policies that expand or reallocate the housing stock, including mortgage interest deduction, rent regulation and planning restrictions. Model integration, calibration and welfare comparisons are ongoing.

Research programme

My future research connects microeconomic information, behavioural mechanisms and evidence for policy evaluation. I will compare register-based model populations with synthetic populations and networks to identify which joint balance-sheet exposures and worker–firm relationships matter for monetary and fiscal transmission, accounting for measurement and linkage errors. The longer-term aim is one agent per real household, linked to housing transactions through ODISSEI and comparable infrastructures abroad.

I will develop estimation methods that combine aggregate time series with independent empirical estimates of household and firm responses, and assess policy choices across specifications consistent with that evidence. In parallel, I will study the interaction between subjective expectations and limited attention, conditioning amortised neural-network solvers on expectation and sparsity parameters. Comparisons in HANK and ABM settings will examine how these mechanisms interact with market adjustment, with later extensions to firms' production and investment decisions.

Software
