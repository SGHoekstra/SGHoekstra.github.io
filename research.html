My research views macroeconomics through the lens of complex systems. I have worked with both heterogeneous-agent equilibrium models and large-scale agent-based models, and my aim is to use the insights of both to better understand macroeconomic phenomena, using theory, large-scale computational modelling and empirical methods.

A Dutch Agent-Based Model with Optimal Consumption

Steven Hoekstra

Job market paper · Working paper · Earlier version circulated as Forecasting with a Dutch Agent-Based Model and Optimally Behaving Households

  • Paper (PDF)
  • Slides
  • Code
Abstract

We develop an extension of the stock-flow consistent agent-based model of Poledna et al. (2023) by introducing the household optimisation problem of Kaplan et al. (2018), which allows for heterogeneous marginal propensities to consume. We use a neural network approach to recalculate the solution of the optimisation problem each period for all Monte Carlo simulations, totalling more than 100,000 re-optimisations. Household agents make optimal choices given their perceived laws of motion, which we model with an AR(1) rule, and update their optimal choices each period as new information arrives. We analyse the model by means of a forecasting exercise in which we compare it to the base version of the agent-based model, a version of the agent-based model with a more complex consumption heuristic, and a benchmark VAR model. We calibrate the model for the Dutch economy and forecast the Dutch economy. The optimal consumption model has better forecast performance than the benchmark VAR, the base agent-based model and the extended-heuristic agent-based model.

Estimation, Forecasting and Parameter Constancy in a Dutch Agent-Based Model

Steven Hoekstra

Draft

Large macroeconomic agent-based models fix their behavioural coefficients by calibration, which places them beyond empirical scrutiny. I recalibrate the CANVAS model for the Netherlands and estimate its demand-pull, cost-push, expectation and wage-indexation pass-through parameters by neural posterior estimation. The estimates imply substantially incomplete pass-through, with firms absorbing a third to a half of cost and demand shocks into margins, so that a ten per cent wage shock produces no wage–price spiral. I also initialise the firm population one to one from Dutch administrative balance-sheet records, and ask whether that richer heterogeneity moves the estimated parameters, the no-spiral conclusion, or forecast accuracy.

Software

  • BeforeIT.jl — contributor
    A high-performance Julia implementation of the macroeconomic agent-based model of Poledna et al. (2023), developed at Banca d'Italia. I use and contribute to the package for my work on the Dutch economy.
