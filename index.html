Steven Hoekstra

PhD Candidate in Economics
University of Amsterdam & Tinbergen Institute

I study how differences in households' and firms' balance sheets, employment risks and expectations shape policy transmission, and when estimated macroeconomic models capture these effects reliably. I combine heterogeneous-agent equilibrium models and agent-based models with microdata, neural-network solvers and simulation-based inference. My three dissertation projects examine optimal household decisions under subjective expectations, the reliability of estimated wage–price transmission, and the welfare costs of housing shortage and misallocation.

Research

  • Forecasting with a Dutch Agent-Based Model and Optimally Behaving Households — with Cars Hommes and Frank Pijpers, working paper
    Two-asset HANK households optimise under subjective expectations inside an ABM. Twelve-quarter aggregate consumption forecast errors are 9.9–21.8% lower than matched univariate benchmarks; portfolio adjustment is an important mechanism. Runner-up, CEF 2026 Graduate Student Paper Award.
  • BeforeIT.jl: High-Performance Agent-Based Macroeconomics Made Easy — research software, with Meligrana, Devetak, Poledna, Reiter and Glielmo
    The Bank of Italy's open-source Julia framework for macroeconomic agent-based modelling. I developed its validation suite and contributed model extensions and forecast-evaluation tools.
  • When Do Estimated Macroeconomic Models Misread Wage–Price Transmission? — single-author working paper; preliminary results
    Known-truth experiments test whether good historical fit can conceal distorted intervention responses, followed by comparisons of alternative specifications for Dutch wage–price transmission.
  • Shortage or Misallocation? The Welfare Loss from Dutch Housing Policy, 2015–2035 — work in progress
    A spatial macroeconomic ABM with housing, labour-market mobility, demography and firm dynamics, designed to compare policies that expand or reallocate the housing stock. Calibration and welfare comparisons are ongoing.
