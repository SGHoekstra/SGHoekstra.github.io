Steven Hoekstra

PhD Candidate in Economics
University of Amsterdam & Tinbergen Institute

I study macroeconomics through the lens of complex systems. I work with both heterogeneous-agent equilibrium models and large-scale agent-based models, and combine theory, high-performance computation and empirical methods to understand macroeconomic dynamics. My current work builds agent-based models of the Dutch economy: one in which households consume optimally, one that estimates the strength of the channels behind inflation, and one that puts an institutionally detailed housing market inside a macroeconomic model.

Research

  • Forecasting with a Dutch Agent-Based Model and Optimally Behaving Households — with Cars Hommes and Frank Pijpers, working paper
    An agent-based model of the Dutch economy in which forward-looking households solve a two-asset consumption–savings problem, which forecasts household consumption better than heuristic versions of the model and time-series benchmarks.
  • BeforeIT.jl: High-Performance Agent-Based Macroeconomics Made Easy — with Meligrana, Devetak, Poledna, Reiter and Glielmo, preprint
    The Bank of Italy's open-source Julia framework for building and simulating macroeconomic agent-based models, and the first industry-grade package of its kind.
  • Estimation, Forecasting and Parameter Constancy in a Dutch Agent-Based Model — draft
    Estimating the pass-through parameters that agent-based models usually fix by calibration, and asking whether the 2022 inflation episode could have ignited a wage–price spiral.
  • An Agent-Based Model of the Housing Market — draft
    A spatially explicit model of the Dutch housing market, used to weigh rent regulation, zoning liberalisation and mortgage interest relief.
