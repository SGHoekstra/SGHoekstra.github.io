Steven Hoekstra

PhD Candidate in Economics
University of Amsterdam & Tinbergen Institute

I study macroeconomics through the lens of complex systems. I work with both heterogeneous-agent equilibrium models and large-scale agent-based models, and combine theory, high-performance computation and empirical methods to understand macroeconomic dynamics. My job market paper builds an agent-based model of the Dutch economy in which households consume optimally, and shows that this improves forecasting performance over heuristic agent-based models and a VAR benchmark.

Job market paper

Finalist, Society for Computational Economics student paper contest, CEF 2025

A Dutch Agent-Based Model with Optimal Consumption

I extend the stock-flow consistent agent-based model of Poledna et al. (2023) with the household optimisation problem of Kaplan, Moll and Violante (2018), which generates heterogeneous marginal propensities to consume. A neural network re-solves the household problem every period of every Monte Carlo run, more than 100,000 re-optimisations in total. Calibrated to the Dutch economy, the model forecasts better than the base agent-based model, a version with a richer consumption heuristic, and a VAR benchmark.
